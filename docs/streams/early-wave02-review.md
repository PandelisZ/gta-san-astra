Decision 1's image shows the front at that curb, and its rationale admits substantial forward travel without useful alignment. Decision 3's image instead shows the rear wheels on the opposite garden sidewalk. The earlier immediate rear corridor was clear; that did not establish clearance for the whole planned travel. This is evidence of a mistaken distance/stopping expectation, not proof that the reverse steering sign or input delivery was wrong. ## At most two next-wave improvements

1. **Tie planned travel to observed clearance.** Before another powered or neutral phase near a boundary, compare recent displacement against remaining clearance along the car's actual diagonal path, including the rear during reverse. Treat “slow,” “short,” and “braked” as unverified until images support them. If the last action consumed substantial clearance without enough heading change, revise the motion expectation before another similar burst. The model still chooses every control and duration.
2. **Separate observed state from intended effect.** Keep route memory explicit about “countersteer requested” versus “road-parallel observed,” and “reverse braking requested” versus “backward travel visibly arrested.” Use two static road features where possible; a moving traffic gap alone cannot prove own-car motion. This would prevent a requested correction or stop from becoming an assumed fact in the next plan.
